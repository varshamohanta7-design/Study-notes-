WATER-SOLUBLE VITAMINS

Meaning & Basic Characteristics: Water-soluble vitamins are vitamins that dissolve readily in water and, according to the study material, are not stored to any significant extent in the body.

Core Characteristics Overview

  • Storage in body: Not stored to any extent in body tissue.
  • Excess intake: Excess is excreted through the kidney.
  • Requirement: Regular / daily supply is necessary for normal body functions.
  • B-group production: Some B vitamins result from bacterial activity in the small intestine.
  • Food use: Many are used as food additives.
  • Cooking loss: Extensively lost by leaching during cooking.

Cooking Observation: LEACHING

Leaching: Movement of water-soluble components from food into the cooking water. Causes massive vitamin loss!

EXAM TIP

Frequently Confused Point: Excess water-soluble vitamins are not stored in fat or liver; they are excreted through the kidney!

B-COMPLEX VITAMINS & CHEMICAL NAMES

Why Grouped Together?

One key reason for grouping B vitamins together is that they occur together in nature.

Key Features of B-Complex:

  1. Occurrence: Occur together in nature.
  2. Nitrogen Content: According to study material, ALL B-complex vitamins contain nitrogen in their structure.
  3. Coenzyme Function: In general, function as coenzymes essential in metabolic processes of living cells.
Conceptual Flow: B-complex vitamins → Function as coenzymes → Participate in metabolic reactions → Essential for cell survival.

VITAMIN B₁ — THIAMINE

Coenzyme: TPP • Disease: Beri-beri

🧪 Solubility & Stability

  • Solubility: Readily soluble in water; insoluble in fat solvents.
  • Stability Status: Described as one of the least stable vitamins.
  • Dry vs Solution: Dry form is fairly stable. Solution form is unstable with heat & alkali.
  • Freezing effect: Freezing has little or no effect on thiamine content.
  • Key Factor: Temperature is a major stability factor.

🔥 Thermal & Chemical Effects

Thermal destruction is pH-dependent!

🍖 "Meaty" Flavour Link: Thermal destruction of thiamine yields degradation products that create characteristic odour & contribute to "meaty" flavour in cooked foods.
  • Cereal Protection: Starch/protein in cereals protect thiamine in certain pH ranges.
  • Destroyed by: Oxidation & Reduction.
  • Sensitivities: Sensitive to Sulphur dioxide (SO₂) & Sulphites. Do NOT use SO₂ to preserve thiamine foods!

⚙️ Function & Active Form

Coenzyme Synthesis

Thiamine (B₁) + Phosphoric Acid
↓
TPP
(Thiamine Pyrophosphate)

Risk Factor: High consumption of tea & alcohol alters thiamine absorption, increasing deficiency risk!

🚨 Vitamin B₁ Deficiency Spectrum

HIGH-YIELD EXAM TOPIC

1. Mild Deficiency Symptoms

  • Fatigue & Emotional instability
  • Depression & Irritability
  • Retarded normal growth
  • Loss of appetite

2. Severe Prolonged Deficiency → BERI-BERI

Affects 3 major body systems:

Neurological System Cardiovascular System Gastrointestinal Tract

Source diagram highlights: Loss of tendon reflexes, foot/wrist drop, tingling, numbness, weakness, emaciation.

VITAMIN B₂ — RIBOFLAVIN

Coenzymes: FMN & FAD • Light Sensitive

🥛 Food Sources & Pigment

Widely distributed in small amounts. Good sources:

Milk Cheese Liver Eggs Leafy Vegetables Dried Yeast (Rich!)

Pigment Group: Belongs to yellow fluorescent pigments called Flavins.
Color: Orange-Yellow → On reduction becomes Colourless.

☀️ Stability & Sensitivity

  • Solubility: Less soluble than thiamine (B₁).
  • Heat Stability: More stable than B₁ in acidic & neutral media.
  • Alkaline Heating: Destroyed by heating in alkali. Baking soda used for faster cooking destroys much riboflavin!
  • Light Sensitivity: On exposure to LIGHT, riboflavin readily loses vitamin activity!

🔄 Coenzymes (FMN & FAD)

Must undergo phosphorylation in intestinal tract before absorption!

FMN & FAD

👁️ Riboflavin (B₂) Deficiency Manifestations

Cheilosis Reddened denuded areas on lips, cracks at corners of mouth.
Glostitis (Spelled Glostitis in source) Swollen & reddened tongue.
Dermatitis Scaly, greasy dermatitis on face, ears & body.

VITAMIN B₃ — NIACIN

Coenzymes: NAD & NADP • Disease: Pellagra (3 Ds)

🧬 Meaning & Dual Synthesis

Niacin Term: Includes Nicotinic acid + Nicotinamide.

Synthesis Pathways:

1. Synthesized by intestinal microflora.

2. Formed in tissues from amino acid TRYPTOPHAN.

*Milk & eggs have high niacin potency due to tryptophan!

🛡️ High Stability (Tough Cookie!)

One of the MOST STABLE vitamins!

Contrast: B₁ is fragile (least stable), B₃ is tough (most stable)!

VITAMIN B₅ — PANTOTHENIC ACID

Coenzyme A (CoA) • Burning Feet Syndrome

🌾 Sources & Synthesis

Widely distributed in foods.

  • Abundant in: Animal tissues, whole-grain cereals, pulses.
  • Lesser amounts in: Milk, vegetables, fruits.
  • Flora synthesis: Synthesized by intestinal microflora.

⚖️ Stability (pH 4 – 7)

Stable pH Range: pH 4 to 7
  • Form: More stable in solution than dry form.
  • Decomposed by: Alkali, Dry heat, Moist heat.
  • Neutral solution: Stable to moist heat in neutral solutions.

🔑 Coenzyme A (CoA)

Coenzyme A

Essential for: Release of energy from carbohydrates & Glucose synthesis.

🔥 Vitamin B₅ Deficiency — BURNING FEET SYNDROME

High Yield
Primary Manifestation: Burning sensation & numbness in feet. Pain & abnormal sensations in arms and legs.
Other Associated Symptoms: Loss of appetite, nausea, indigestion, pulse rise, fainting attacks, & increased susceptibility to infection.
