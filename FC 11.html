1. VITAMINS — INTRODUCTION

1.1 Definition: Vitamins are essential organic micronutrients vital for normal body functioning.

  • They are Organic Compounds.
  • Required by the body in small amounts.
  • Essential for normal metabolic and enzyme-related body functions.
  • Distinct from Macronutrients: (Carbohydrates, Proteins, Fats).

2. CLASSIFICATION OF VITAMINS

Topper's Sheet #2

Vitamins are classified strictly on the basis of their SOLUBILITY into two major groups:

VITAMINS
1. Water-Soluble W.B.C.
  • • B-Complex vitamins
  • • Vitamin C
🧠 Trick: W.B.C. = Water = Vitamin B + Vitamin C
2. Fat-Soluble A D E K
  • • Vitamin A, D, E, K
🧠 Trick: ADEK = Fat Soluble Group
🔴 EXAM ALERT: B-COMPLEX STRUCTURE

B-complex is NOT a single compound. It consists of several chemically unrelated compounds, all of which contain NITROGEN as part of their chemical structure!

4. BROAD FUNCTIONAL ROLES OF VITAMINS

⚙️

1. Coenzymes

Water-soluble vitamins participate directly in enzyme reactions as coenzymes for proper metabolic pathways.

⚖️

2. Metabolic Regulators

Regulate and maintain smooth metabolic processes throughout the cell.

🦴

3. Structural Roles

Contribute to structural components like Bones & Teeth.

5. EXCESS VITAMINS: EXCRETION vs TOXICITY

The fate of excess vitamins depends entirely on their solubility group:

EXCESS VITAMINS IN BODY
Water-Soluble (B, C)

➔ Exceeding metabolic needs?

TYPICALLY EXCRETED in Urine 🚽

Low risk of toxicity accumulation

Fat-Soluble (A, D, E, K)

➔ Exceeding metabolic needs?

STORED IN BODY (Fat tissue) 📦
⚠️ ACCUMULATION ➔ POTENTIAL TOXICITY!
🧠 FREQUENTLY CONFUSED POINT:

Do not assume all vitamins are stored or excreted the same! Water-soluble vitamins pass in urine easily, while fat-soluble vitamins store in body fats and cannot be excreted in urine according to source material, posing hypervitaminosis/toxicity risk.

9. LOSSES IN FOOD PROCESSING & 10. RESTORATION

Topper's Sheet #5

9. VITAMIN LOSSES DURING PROCESSING & STORAGE

1. Trimming & Peeling Examples

Specific exam examples from source:

  • Apple & Pineapple ➔ Vitamin C Loss
  • Carrot ➔ Vitamin B₃ Loss

2. Cereal Milling

Cereals hold vitamins in their Seed Coat & Germ.

➔ Milling removes seed coat + germ, leading to major vitamin depletion!

Other Causes of Vitamin Depletion:

Heating & Blanching: Removes water-soluble vitamins.
HTST Processing & Chemicals: High Temperature Short Time degrades sensitive vitamins.
Storage: Prolonged storage causes oxidation/degradation.

10. RESTORATION OF VITAMINS

When vitamins are lost during processing, they are restored back into foods via:

1. FORTIFICATION

Adding vitamins to foods to boost nutrient levels.

2. ENRICHMENT

Replenishing specific nutrients lost during processing.

11. FAT-SOLUBLE VITAMINS — DEEP DIVE

Associated Foods: Butter, Cream, Vegetable oils, Meat fats, Fish fats.

Nitrogen Status: Contain NO Nitrogen in their structure (unlike B-complex!).

Heat Stability: More stable to heat than B vitamins (Less cooking loss!).

Absorption: Absorbed in intestine along with dietary fats and lipids.
