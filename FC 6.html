1. PROTEINS & DIGESTION

► DEFINITION OF PROTEINS

  • Proteins are macromolecules made up of 20 different amino acids.
  • On hydrolysis, proteins break down completely into amino acids.
  • Amino acids are the structural units (building blocks) of proteins.

► STRUCTURE OF AN AMINO ACID

Every amino acid contains 3 essential functional groups attached to the α (alpha) carbon:

  1. Amino group (–NH₂) ➡ Basic Group
  2. Carboxyl group (–COOH) ➡ Acidic Group
  3. Side chain (R group) ➡ Determines Identity & Properties

*All three groups + a hydrogen atom are covalently bonded to the central α-carbon.

General Structure of α-Amino Acid

► 2. DIGESTION OF PROTEINS

During digestion, complex proteins are broken down step-by-step via hydrolysis:

PROTEIN Complex Polymer
➔
POLYPEPTIDE >10 Amino Acids
➔
PEPTIDE Short Chains
➔
AMINO ACIDS Final End Product!
  • Hydrolysis occurs through proteolytic enzymes (proteases).
  • Final products: Free Amino Acids (absorbable by the body).
💡 EXAM TIP / KEY POINT Must Remember

Always mention that hydrolysis of proteins specifically uses Proteolytic Enzymes, and the end product is ALWAYS Amino Acids!

2. PRIMARY STRUCTURE & PEPTIDE BOND

► 3. PRIMARY STRUCTURE OF PROTEIN

  • Definition: The primary structure is the linear sequence of amino acids in a protein chain.
  • Amino acids join together in long, continuous chains.
  • Connected by covalent peptide bonds.
  • CRUCIAL POINT: The specific order of amino acids determines the overall nature and function of the protein.

► 4. PEPTIDE BOND FORMATION

A peptide bond is formed by condensation reaction between:

Amino group (–NH₂) of one amino acid + Carboxyl group (–COOH) of another amino acid. *(Elimination of 1 molecule of H₂O)*

Classification / Types of Peptides:

Dipeptide

2 Amino Acids

1 Peptide Bond
Oligopeptide

3 to 10 Amino Acids

2–9 Peptide Bonds
Polypeptide

> 10 Amino Acids

Many Peptide Bonds

Amino Acid 3-Letter 1-Letter

► 6. PROTEIN SYNTHESIS

The amino acid sequence is strictly encoded in DNA. Occurs in 2 major steps:

Step A: TRANSCRIPTION

DNA genetic code is used as a template to produce messenger RNA (mRNA) inside the nucleus.

DNA ➔ mRNA
Step B: TRANSLATION

mRNA acts as a template. Ribosomes read mRNA to synthesize the exact amino acid chain (protein).

mRNA + Ribosome ➔ Protein
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE

Don't confuse Transcription and Translation!
• Transcription = Making mRNA from DNA.
• Translation = Making Protein from mRNA at the Ribosome!

3. SECONDARY, TERTIARY & QUATERNARY STRUCTURES

► 7. SECONDARY STRUCTURE OF PROTEIN

  • Definition: Local folding of the polypeptide chain.
  • Mainly depends on Hydrogen Bonding!
  • Two major forms: α (Alpha) helix & β (Beta) sheet.
🌀 α (Alpha) Helix
  • Right-handed coiled helical structure.
  • Side chains (R groups) project outward.
  • Stabilized by intramolecular hydrogen bonds.
H-Bond forms between:
• Carbonyl oxygen (C=O) of one amino acid
• Hydrogen of N–H group (N–H) 4 residues ahead!
📄 β (Beta) Sheet
  • Consists of extended polypeptide strands lying side-by-side.
  • Hydrogen bonds form between adjacent strands (interchain/interstrand).
Parallel β-sheet

Strands run in same direction

Antiparallel β-sheet

⭐ MORE STABLE!

*Antiparallel is more stable because its H-bonds are better aligned linearly!

► 8. TERTIARY STRUCTURE

  • Definition: The overall three-dimensional (3D) shape of a single polypeptide chain.
  • Protein bends and twists to achieve maximum stability and lowest energy state.
  • Stabilized by interactions among amino acid side chains (R groups).
  • Classic Example: MYOGLOBIN

► 9. QUATERNARY STRUCTURE

  • Definition: Present ONLY in proteins containing two or more polypeptide chains (subunits).
  • Chains may be identical or different.
  • Held together by non-covalent, weak bonds/interactions.
  • Represents spatial association of multiple polypeptide subunits.
  • Classic Example: HAEMOGLOBIN (4 subunits)

SUMMARY: Hierarchy of Protein Organization

1° Primary

Linear AA sequence joined by peptide bonds.

2° Secondary

α-Helix / β-Sheet stabilized by H-bonds.

3° Tertiary

Overall 3D folding of 1 chain. (e.g. Myoglobin)

4° Quaternary

Association of 2+ chains. (e.g. Haemoglobin)

🧠 MEMORY TRICK (Examples)

Remember the 3D vs 4D Examples:
• Myoglobin = 1 Chain = 3° Structure
• Haemoglobin = 4 Subunits = 4° Structure
