1 VITAMIN A — RETINOL

Anti-Infective Vitamin 👁️

1.1 Chemical Identity

  • Vitamin A is chemically an Alcohol (Retinol).
  • Called Retinol due to its specific function in the retina of the eye.
  • Metabolically Active Forms:
    • • Retinol → Alcohol form
    • • Retinal → Corresponding Aldehyde form
    • • Retinoic acid → Corresponding Acid form

1.2 Provitamin A — Carotenoids

Carotenoids are plant precursors (provitamins). Structurally, they are unsaturated hydrocarbons.

Four Important Carotenoids:

α-carotene β-carotene (Most Important!) γ-carotene Cryptoxanthin

⭐ HIGH-YIELD EXAM FACT:

β-carotene has a symmetrical structure. Upon symmetrical cleavage in the body:

1 molecule of β-Carotene ➔ 2 molecules of Vitamin A

1.3 Stability & Destruction

  • Fairly stable to moderate heat.
  • Prolonged heating in contact with air destroys Vitamin A!
  • Easily destroyed by Oxidation and UV Light.
  • Protection: Antioxidants prevent oxidative loss of Vitamin A.
Diagram 1.1

β-Carotene Cleavage Scheme

Symmetrical cleavage yields exactly 2 active retinol units.

👁️ Role in Vision & Rhodopsin

• The retina contains two photosensitive cell types:

RODS 🌙

• Night / Low Light

• Pigment: Rhodopsin (Visual Purple)

CONES ☀️

• Day / Bright Light

• Color Vision

RHODOPSIN = OPSIN (Protein) + RETINAL (Aldehyde)
Why "Anti-Infective Vitamin"?

Vitamin A maintains health of epithelial cells & mucous membranes. Mucous traps pathogens. Deficiency causes keratinization, weakening defence barriers!

Vitamin A Deficiency Disorders

1. Night Blindness

Failure of rods to regenerate rhodopsin quickly in low light. Inability to adapt to darkness.

2. Xerophthalmia

Corneal keratinization making eyes dry & opaque. Leading cause of childhood blindness in developing nations.

3. Xeroderma

Skin becomes excessively dry, scaly, rough, and toad-like due to lost mucous secretion capability.

2 VITAMIN D — CALCIFEROL

Sunshine Vitamin 🦴

⭐ Exam Comparison - Stability:

Unlike Vitamin A (which is easily oxidized), Vitamin D is remarkably stable! Storage, food warming, processing, and cooking do NOT cause significant loss.

2.2 Biochemical Functions

  • Increases intestinal absorption of calcium.
  • Promotes phosphate transport across intestine.
  • Increases renal reabsorption of Ca²⁺ in kidneys.
  • Maintains proper serum Calcium & Phosphorus levels.
  • Essential for mineralization & healthy growth of bones & teeth.

⚠️ Rickets vs. Osteomalacia HIGH-YIELD

3 VITAMIN E — TOCOPHEROL

Master Antioxidant 🛡️

3.1 Sources & Most Active Form

  • Chemical Name: Tocopherol.
  • Most active biological form: α-Tocopherol.
  • Richest Source: Wheat Germ Oil.
  • Other sources: Vegetable oils, nuts, cereals, green plants, egg yolk, butter.

3.2 Antioxidant Mechanism

The paramount function of Vitamin E is its Antioxidant Property:

1. Prevents Peroxide Formation:

Inhibits peroxidation of Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids (PUFAs) in membranes.

2. Cell Membrane Protection:

Shields lipid bilayers from free radical oxidative damage.

3. Link to Ageing:

Since ageing is characterized by membrane deterioration, Vitamin E helps slow oxidative breakdown.

📊 High-Yield Numerical & Food Processing Facts:

  • Whole Wheat Processing: ≥ 80% Vitamin E activity is lost when whole wheat is converted into refined white bread!
  • Dehydration: Dehydration reduces Vitamin E activity due to increased oxidation.
  • Toxicity & Interactions: Vitamin E is relatively non-toxic compared to Vit A & D. However, very high doses may interferes with Vitamin K blood clotting activity!

Oxidative Protection Flowchart

🚨 Vitamin E Deficiency Manifestations:

  • RBC Fragility: Increased sensitivity of red blood cell membranes to rupture (haemolysis).
  • Muscle Damage: Dystrophy/weakness of skeletal muscle tissue.
  • Early Ageing: Accelerated cell membrane oxidative deterioration.

4 VITAMIN K — ANTI-HAEMORRHAGIC

Koagulation Factor 🩸

4.1 Naturally Occurring Forms

Vitamin K₁

Phylloquinone

Primary Source: Green leafy plants 🥬

Vitamin K₂

Menaquinone

Origin: Synthesized by Intestinal Gut Bacteria 🦠

⚖️ Origin Ratio in Humans (~50:50):

Approximately 50% of Vitamin K in human bodies is synthesized internally by intestinal microflora (Menaquinone), while the remaining 50% is dietary (Phylloquinone).

4.2 Biological Function & Blood Clotting

Vitamin K is essential for hepatic synthesis of Prothrombin (Factor II) and other clotting factors.

Vitamin K ➔ Prothrombin Synthesis ➔ Normal Blood Clotting
👶 Why are Newborn Infants Susceptible?

Deficiency is uncommon in normal adults due to gut bacteria synthesis. However, newborn infants lack established intestinal bacterial flora and have low prothrombin levels, making them highly vulnerable to haemorrhagic disease of the newborn!

🚨 Vitamin K Deficiency Symptoms:

  • Prolonged Blood Clotting Time ⏱️
  • Internal & Subcutaneous Haemorrhage 🩸
  • Uncontrolled bleeding from minor wounds

7. ASSERTION-REASON TYPE REVISION

Set 1: Vitamin A & Night Vision

Assertion (A): Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness.

Reason (R): Vitamin A (retinal) is required for the regeneration of rhodopsin pigment in retinal rod cells.

✅ Explanation: Both A and R are True, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Set 2: Vitamin D Bone Manifestations

Assertion (A): Vitamin D deficiency produces the identical bone manifestation in both children and adults.

Reason (R): Vitamin D regulates serum calcium and phosphate levels.

❌ Explanation: Assertion (A) is FALSE! In children it causes Rickets (bending pliable ends), whereas in adults it causes Osteomalacia (decalcification/fractures). Reason (R) is TRUE.
Set 3: Vitamin E Antioxidant Protection

Assertion (A): Vitamin E helps protect cell membranes from oxidative deterioration.

Reason (R): Vitamin E prevents the formation of peroxides from polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs).

✅ Explanation: Both A and R are True, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Set 4: Vitamin K Bleeding Vulnerability

Assertion (A): Newborn infants are prone to Vitamin K deficiency bleeding.

Reason (R): Intestinal microflora that synthesizes Menaquinone (Vitamin K₂) is not yet established in newborns.

✅ Explanation: Both A and R are True, and R correctly explains why newborns are at high risk.
