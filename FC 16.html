1. MICRO-MINERALS / TRACE MINERALS

DEFINITION: Micro-minerals (also called trace minerals) are essential minerals required by the body in amounts of < 20 mg per day.

💡 General Importance:

  • Their absence directly leads to deficiency diseases.
  • Function as crucial components of enzymes and organic compounds.
  • Examples of mineral-containing organic compounds: Haemoglobin (contains Iron) & Thyroxine (contains Iodine).

📊 Classification Tree of Micro-Minerals

Major Micro-Minerals

Iron (Fe) • Fluoride (F) • Zinc (Zn) • Copper (Cu) • Iodine (I) • Chromium (Cr) • Cobalt (Co)

⇄

Other Micro-Minerals

Silicon (Si) • Vanadium (V) • Tin (Sn) • Selenium (Se) • Nickel (Ni) • Molybdenum (Mo)

2. IRON (Fe) — Complete Breakdown

Iron is one of the most vital trace elements discussed in food chemistry.

A. Amount and Distribution in the Body:

• Total body iron in a healthy adult = 3 – 5 g.

Form Approx. Proportion Major Location / Biological Form
Functional Iron ~ 70% Haemoglobin, Myoglobin & Oxidation-Reduction Enzymes
Storage Iron ~ 30% Liver, Bone Marrow & Spleen (as Ferritin & Haemosiderin)

⭐ EXAM MUST-REMEMBER FACT:

70% Functional Iron : 30% Storage Iron

(Storage sites: Liver, Bone Marrow, Spleen as Ferritin & Haemosiderin)

B. Functions of Iron in the Human Body:

  1. Component of Haemoglobin: Transports O₂ from lungs → tissues and CO₂ from cells → lungs.
  2. Oxygen Transfer within Cells: Present in pigments and intracellular tissue enzymes.
  3. Other Biochemical Roles:
    • Conversion of β-carotene → Vitamin A.
    • Purine synthesis inside cells.
    • Clearance of lipids from blood.
    • Detoxification of drugs in the liver.

🥕 β-Carotene → Vit A
🧬 Purine Synthesis

3. IRON DEFICIENCY & ANAEMIA

⚠️ CRITICAL CONCEPT: Iron deficiency does NOT immediately cause a drop in Haemoglobin!

📉 Exact Sequence of Iron Deficiency Development:

Iron Deficiency Begins
↓
Reduction in Stored Iron (Ferritin/Haemosiderin ↓)
↓
Storage Iron Completely Depleted
↓
Reduction in Hb Concentration + Reduction in RBC Size
↓
MICROCYTIC HYPOCHROMIC ANAEMIA 🩸

🔍 Microcytic:

Associated with significantly smaller red blood cells than normal.

🎨 Hypochromic:

Associated with reduced haemoglobin concentration (paler color).

4. IRON ABSORPTION, RECYCLING & SOURCES

⚡ Absorbability Rule: Fe²⁺ (Ferrous) > Fe³⁺ (Ferric)

• Ferrous iron (Fe²⁺) salts are much more easily absorbed than Ferric iron (Fe³⁺) salts.

✨ Factors Enhancing Iron Absorption:

1. Ascorbic Acid (Vit C) 2. Calcium 3. Growth Phase 4. Pregnancy

🔄 Iron Recycling & Metabolism:

When red blood cells disintegrate:

RBC Disintegration → Hb Breakdown → Non-protein Portion → HAEMATIN (contains Fe) + BILIRUBIN (pigment)

*Almost ALL iron is saved and efficiently reused for synthesis of new red blood corpuscles!

🥗 Sources of Dietary Iron:

  • Richest Sources: Organ meats (Liver, Kidney, Heart).
  • Good Sources: Lean meat, eggs, pulses, nuts, dried fruits, whole grains, green leafy vegetables.
🚫 VERY IMPORTANT NEGATIVE FACT: Milk and milk products are practically devoid of iron!

5. FLUORINE / FLUORIDE (F⁻)

• Occurrence: Widespread in soils, water supplies, plants, and animals. Severe deficiency is rare.
• Storage Sites: Maintained predominantly in Bone and Tooth Enamel.

⚠️ Dose-Dependent Effects of Fluoride:

1.5 – 4 mg/day → Protects against Dental Caries 🦷
≥ 2 mg/L in water → Associated with Teeth Mottling in children
> 20 mg/day (chronic) → Chronic Toxicity = FLUOROSIS

6. ZINC (Zn)

  • Biochemical Functions: Zinc-containing enzymes participate in Nucleic Acid Synthesis & Degradation.
  • Storage: Stored in large amounts in Bones.
  • Adult Recommended Intake: 15 mg / day.
  • Inhibitors of Zinc Availability: Phytic Acid and Fibre adversely affect zinc absorption.
  • Sources: Animal meat & seafood offer much higher bio-available zinc than vegetable products.

🚨 Signs of Zinc Deficiency:

Loss of appetite • Failure to grow • Skin changes • Impaired wound regeneration • Low immunity • Infertility • Decreased taste activity (hypogeusia)

7. COPPER (Cu)

• Transport Form: Combines with plasma protein Ceruloplasmin (transportable form).

• Adult Requirement: 2 – 3 mg / day (met by normal daily diet).

• Food Sources: Oysters, nuts, liver, kidney, dried pulses.

⚠️ EXAM TRAP: Copper Deficiency Anaemia

Copper deficiency leads to anaemia NOT because of dietary iron lack, but because of improper mobilization of iron from iron storage sites!

8. IODINE (I)

• Function: Integral component of the thyroid hormone Thyroxin.

• Deficiency Condition: Enlargement of the thyroid gland = GOITRE.

• Sources & Prevention: Excellent natural source = Seafoods. Prevented/corrected via Iodized Salt containing Potassium Iodate or Potassium Iodide.

9. CHROMIUM (Cr)

  • Function: Essential for normal Glucose Metabolism. Absence causes disturbed glucose metabolism.
  • Special Observation: Chromium levels in human tissues decline with age.
  • Sources: Brewer's yeast, meat products, cheese, whole grains, spices.

10. COBALT (Co)

⭐ DIRECT EXAM STATEMENT: Cobalt deficiency has NEVER been described in human beings!

• Major Role: Forms an integral part of Vitamin B₁₂ (Cobalamin).

• Microbial Synthesis: Inorganic cobalt is needed for microbial B₁₂ synthesis in animal gut. According to the PDF, microbial synthesis allows true vegetarians to live for years without dietary B₁₂.

11. SELENIUM (Se)

  • Regional Variation: Soil selenium varies greatly by region → directly affects food content.
  • Antioxidant Role: Acts as a potent Antioxidant and enhances Tocopherol (Vit E) activity.
  • Enzyme Association: Integral constituent of the enzyme Glutathione Peroxidase.

12. MOLYBDENUM (Mo)

  • Enzyme Component: Constituent of Aldehyde Oxidase and Xanthine Oxidase.
  • Food Processing Note: Bacterial nitrate involved in meat curing and pickling contains molybdenum.

13. NICKEL (Ni)

  • Enzyme Activator: Activates Alkaline Phosphatase and Oxalacetate Decarboxylase (can also be activated by other divalent ions).
  • Hormonal Effect: Directly enhances Insulin activity.

26. MIXED PRACTICE & ASSERTION-REASON SET

Test your knowledge directly against TOPPER-level exam questions! Click "Show Answer" to reveal solutions.

Q1. Micro-minerals are generally required by the body in amounts:

A. > 100 mg/day B. 50 – 100 mg/day C. < 20 mg/day D. > 1 g/day
Correct Answer: C (< 20 mg/day) — By definition, micro-minerals/trace minerals are required in amounts less than 20 mg per day.

Q2. What type of anaemia is produced by prolonged iron deficiency?

A. Macrocytic normochromic B. Microcytic hypochromic C. Sickle-cell anaemia D. Haemolytic anaemia
Correct Answer: B (Microcytic hypochromic anaemia) — Microcytic = smaller cells, Hypochromic = reduced Hb concentration.

Q3. Which plasma protein forms the transportable form of copper?

A. Haemoglobin B. Ceruloplasmin C. Ferritin D. Transferrin
Correct Answer: B (Ceruloplasmin) — Copper combines with ceruloplasmin for transport in blood.

Q4. Which enzyme contains Selenium as an integral component?

A. Xanthine oxidase B. Glutathione peroxidase C. Alkaline phosphatase D. Aldehyde oxidase
Correct Answer: B (Glutathione peroxidase) — Selenium acts as an antioxidant inside glutathione peroxidase.

Q5 (Assertion–Reason):

Assertion (A): Fe²⁺ is more easily absorbed than Fe³⁺.

Reason (R): Ferrous salts are identified as more readily absorbed than ferric salts in food chemistry.

Answer: Both A and R are True, and R correctly explains A. (Fe²⁺ > Fe³⁺ in ease of absorption).
