MINERALS — BASIC CONCEPT

📌 Definition: Minerals are inorganic components of food that leave ash as residue when burnt.

How do minerals occur in food & tissues?

  • A. As inorganic salts:
    • Sodium chloride → NaCl
    • Calcium phosphate
  • B. In combination with organic compounds:
    • Phosphoproteins
    • Phospholipids
    • Haemoglobins

Chief Ionic Forms:

Na⁺ → Sodium
K⁺ → Potassium
Ca²⁺ → Calcium
Cl⁻ → Chloride
H₂PO₄⁻ → Dihydrogen phosphate

REMEMBER:

Minerals are strictly inorganic. Their presence is detected as ASH after complete burning!

2

IMPORTANCE AND FUNCTIONS OF MINERALS

4–6%

of total human body weight consists of minerals.

~80%

of total body mineral is stored in the skeleton.

Major Biological Functions:

1. Regulation of enzyme metabolism
2. Maintenance of acid–base balance
3. Maintenance of osmotic pressure
4. Facilitation of membrane transport of essential compounds
5. Maintenance of nerve functioning
6. Maintenance of muscular functioning
⚠️ EXAM ALERT

Small requirement ≠ Harmless in excess!

Although trace minerals are required in very small quantities, excessive consumption of trace elements can be toxic.

CALCIUM (Ca) — IMPORTANCE & DISTRIBUTION

Abundance

Most abundant mineral in human body

70-kg Adult Male

~1200 g

Total body calcium

Share of Minerals

~39%

Of total body minerals

🦴 Distribution of Body Calcium

Skeleton ~99%
  • Occurs as calcium phosphate in bones.
  • Forms major structural framework.
Fluids & Soft Tissues ~1%
  • Mostly present in ionic form (Ca²⁺).
  • Essential for enzyme activity, blood clotting & muscle contraction.
6 & 7

CALCIUM ABSORPTION & FOOD SOURCES

📊 Usual Absorption Rate: Only about 20–30% of ingested calcium is actually absorbed by the body. Absorption is influenced by Ca-P levels.

Factors Increasing Ca²⁺ Absorption

  • Vitamin D ↑ Increases
  • Low pH (Acidic medium) ↑ Increases
  • Lactose ↑ Increases
  • Low fat ↑ Increases
  • High protein ↑ Increases

Factors Decreasing Ca²⁺ Absorption

  • Oxalic acid (fruits & veggies) ↓ Decreases
  • Phytic acid (cereals) ↓ Decreases
  • Alkaline medium ↓ Decreases
  • Emotional instability ↓ Decreases
⭐ EXAM POINT
Calcium in Milk

Readily assimilated because of the presence of:
1. Lactose + 2. Vitamin D

Other Rich Food Sources:
Molasses Nuts Sesame seeds Ragi (Millet)
8 & 9

FUNCTIONS OF CALCIUM & OSTEOPOROSIS

🦴 Key Biological Functions of Ca:

  1. Bone & teeth formation: Major structural component.
  2. Blood clotting: Participates in coagulation.
    Prothrombin → Thrombin (via Ca²⁺)
  3. Muscle function: Essential for muscle contraction.
  4. Nerve signalling: Participates in signal propagation.
  5. Intracellular messenger: Acts as secondary messenger.
  6. Hormone release: Involved in release of Insulin, PTH, Calcitonin.
  7. Membrane integrity & permeability: Maintains cell boundaries.
  8. Enzyme activation: Specifically activates: Pancreatic lipase, ATPase, and Succinate dehydrogenase.

⚠️ Deficiency Condition: Osteoporosis

Associated with gradual demineralization of bony tissues due to decreased calcium absorption in bones.

1. Porosity of bones
2. Thinness of bones
3. Fragility of bones

PHOSPHORUS (P)

Abundance

2nd most abundant mineral after Calcium.

~22% of total body minerals

Dietary Status

Present in nearly all foods.

Dietary deficiency is VERY RARE!

🧪 Distribution of Phosphorus in the Body

80% In Bones 80%

Present as calcium phosphate in skeletal tissue.

20% In Blood & Cells 20%
  • Soluble phosphate ions
  • Lipids & Proteins
  • Carbohydrates
  • Nucleic acids & Cell membranes
Rich Food Sources:

Meat, Poultry, Fish, Egg, Milk, Nuts, Pulses.

🧠 Memory Hook for P Deficiency:

P deficiency → Weak + Won't eat + Bone pain

(Weakness, Anorexia, Pain in bones)

SULPHUR (S)

⭐ EXAM ALERT

Sulphur differs from other minerals! Required principally in the form of sulphur-containing amino acids.

Sulphur Amino Acid #1

CYSTEINE

Sulphur Amino Acid #2

METHIONINE

Distribution & Biological Association:

  • Present in all proteins.
  • Particularly prevalent in Keratin of skin and hair.
  • Essential component of vitamins: Thiamine (B1) and Biotin (B7)
Food Sources:
Egg Garlic Onions Broccoli
Deficiency Symptoms Mentioned:
  • Severe arthritis symptoms
  • Acne
  • Brittle nails
  • Brittle hair
16–17

MAGNESIUM (Mg)

🌿 Role in Plants:

Component of Chlorophyll.
Absolutely essential for plants to synthesise carbohydrates during photosynthesis!

🧍 Amount in Human Body:

20–28 g

More than half of body magnesium is stored in the bones.

Major Functions:

  • Essential part of enzymes involved in liberation of energy.
  • Helps maintain electrical potential of nerve and muscle membranes.
  • Widely occurs in foods of vegetable origin.
⚠️ Mg Deficiency Associated Complications:
1. Kwashiorkor 2. Hypertension 3. Coronary Heart Disease
18–25

SODIUM (Na), POTASSIUM (K) & CHLORINE (Cl)

Discussed together due to crucial roles in body fluids, membrane potentials & nerve impulse conduction.

Sodium (Na⁺)

2%

Location: Extracellular

Role: Membrane potential + Nerve impulse (with K⁺)

Digestive Fluid: NaHCO₃ in Bile & Pancreatic Juice

Deficiency: Loss of energy, confusion

Potassium (K⁺)

5%

Location: Mainly Intracellular

Role: Membrane potential + Lean body tissue component

Dietary Status: Widely distributed (Deficiency rare)

Deficiency: Muscular weakness, paralysis

Chlorine (Cl⁻)

3%

Location: Extracellular

Role: Digestive fluids balance

Digestive Fluid: HCl in Gastric Juice

Deficiency: Loss of energy, confusion

🧪 Role of Na & Cl in Digestive Fluids

NaCl Daily Limit: ≤ 5 g/day
Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃):
  • Bile
  • Pancreatic juice
Hydrochloric Acid (HCl):
  • Gastric juice
