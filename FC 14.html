PART I

VITAMIN B₆ (PYRIDOXINE)

Light Sensitive!

1. Basic Identity & Chemical Forms

Vitamin B₆ exists in foods as three chemical compounds having Vitamin B₆ activity:

Compound Key Point & Characteristics
Pyridoxine Also called pyridoxol; most stable form
Pyridoxal One of the active forms possessing B₆ activity
Pyridoxamine One of the active forms possessing B₆ activity

⭐ MOST IMPORTANT EXAM FACT:

Pyridoxine (pyridoxol) is the most stable form of Vitamin B₆ and is the form strictly used for food fortification!

2. Environmental Stability

  • Acidic Medium: Stable
  • Alkaline Solution: Relatively stable
  • Light Exposure: VERY UNSTABLE!
⚠️ WATCH OUT
B₆ + Light → Photoconversion → Biologically Inactive Product

Light is the major factor causing direct loss of biological activity of Vitamin B₆.

3. Sources & Distribution

Widely distributed in plant & animal kingdoms.

🧠 Source Recall Trick:
B₆ → Meat + Liver (Best!) + Vegetables + Grain Cereals (with Bran)

4. Active Coenzyme Form

Biologically Active Form = Pyridoxal Phosphate (PLP)

Functions as coenzyme in 3 major nutrient metabolisms:

1. Carbohydrates 2. Fats 3. Proteins

⭐ Key Metabolic Reactions (Exam Hotspots)

🔹 Conversion: Tryptophan → Niacin

Requires PLP as cofactor!

🔹 Glucose Release: Glycogen → Glucose

Enzymatic release of glucose from glycogen reserves.

EXAM TRIGGER

Vitamin B₆ Deficiency Manifestations

1. Depression 2. Confusion 3. Convulsions
B₆ Practice Questions (Quick Self-Test)
Q1-Q4: Forms, Stability & Active State

Q1. 3 B₆-active compounds? → Pyridoxine, Pyridoxal, Pyridoxamine.

Q2. Most stable form? → Pyridoxine (pyridoxol).

Q3. Form used for fortification? → Pyridoxine.

Q4. Active cellular form? → Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP).

Q5-Q8: Functions, Light & Deficiency

Q5. Nutrient metabolisms involved? → Carbohydrates, fats, and proteins.

Q6. Tryptophan to niacin conversion vitamin? → Vitamin B₆.

Q7. Environmental factor causing instability? → Light.

Q8. 3 Deficiency manifestations? → Depression, confusion, and convulsions.

PART II

VITAMIN B₇ (BIOTIN)

Sulphur Containing

1. Key Chemical Features

  • ✨ Biotin = Vitamin B₇
  • 💧 Water-Soluble Vitamin
  • 🟡 SULPHUR-CONTAINING Vitamin

⚠️ Oxidation Susceptibility

Why is Biotin susceptible to oxidation?

Biotin contains a Sulphur Atom → Makes it highly susceptible to oxidation!

2. Biotin Stability Profile & pH Range

Heat STABLE
Light STABLE
Strong Acid UNSTABLE
Strong Alkali UNSTABLE
⭐ OPTIMUM STABILITY RANGE: pH 5 – 8 (High-Yield Numerical Fact!)

3. Good Sources of Biotin

  • Liver & Kidney (Organs)
  • Egg Yolk
  • Groundnuts
  • Some Vegetables

4. Enzymatic Function

Coenzyme for Carboxylating Enzymes!

Capacity to: Add or remove Carbon Dioxide (CO₂).

Major metabolic roles: Carbohydrate & Fat Metabolism

🧠 Biotin Deficiency Recall: Hair + Skin + Nails

1. Hair Loss
2. Scaly Skin
3. Weak Nails
4. Brittle Nails
PART III

FOLIC ACID (VITAMIN B₉)

Folium = Leaf

1. Origin of Name & First Isolation

Derived from Latin word folium = Leaf / Foliage.

🌿 First Isolated From: Spinach Leaves • Widely distributed in green leafy plants.

2. Source Classification

Rich Sources: Yeast, Kidney, Liver, Green Leafy Vegetables.

Top Green Sources: Spinach, Asparagus, Broccoli

Good Sources: Dried beans, Whole wheat bread.

3. Processing & Storage Loss

Folic acid activity is lost in processed and stored foods (e.g. processed milk).

🔥 PRIMARY CAUSE OF INACTIVATION:
OXIDATION

4. Cellular Functions & Cell Division

After absorption, folic acid is converted into active coenzyme forms essential for:

🧬 Nucleic Acid Synthesis
🧪 Normal Amino Acid Metabolism
⭐ High-Yield Link: Nucleic Acid Synthesis + Amino Acid Metabolism → Cell Division & Protein Synthesis!

5. Folic Acid Deficiency Manifestations

🔴 Megaloblastic Anaemia (Major manifestation - large abnormal RBCs)
👶 Poor Growth & Other Blood Disorders
🤢 GI Tract Disturbances (from impaired absorption)
🤰 Pregnancy Note: Folate controls Macrocytic Anaemia during pregnancy!

PART IV

COBALAMIN (VITAMIN B₁₂)

Contains Cobalt

1. Coordination Complex & Forms

Called Cobalamin because it forms a coordination complex with Cobalt at the molecule's centre.

Cyanide Group Attached:
Cyanocobalamin More Stable Form!
Hydroxyl Group Attached:
Hydroxycobalamin

3. Sources (Lecture Specific)

Found ONLY in Animal Foods!

Excellent: Liver, Kidney

Good: Milk, Muscle meat, Cheese, Eggs, Seafood

4. Requirement & Gut Bacteria

Human B₁₂ requirement is very low.

Requirement met by Intestinal Bacterial Synthesis.

5. Pasteurization Loss

Heat sensitive coenzyme form in food.

⭐ Pasteurization of Milk:
~10% Loss of B₁₂

Pernicious Anaemia & Intrinsic Factor (IF) Mechanism

⭐ What is Pernicious Anaemia?

An autoimmune condition that prevents the body from absorbing Vitamin B₁₂. B₁₂ deficiency is usually NOT dietary in origin, but stems from absorption failure.

⭐ Role of Intrinsic Factor (IF):

  • Special protein released by Stomach Cells.
  • Binds Vitamin B₁₂ in gut.
  • Enables intestinal absorption.
Stomach Cells ↓ Intrinsic Factor (IF) → Binds B₁₂ → Intestinal Absorption → Normal Metabolism (GI, Bone Marrow, Nerve Tissue)
PART V

ASCORBIC ACID (VITAMIN C)

Most Unstable!

1. Chemical Properties

Highly soluble compound with:

  • Acidic Properties
  • Strong Reducing Properties

2. Stability Warning

MOST UNSTABLE OF ALL KNOWN VITAMINS!

In solution, easily oxidized. Oxidation accelerated by:

1. Heat 2. Copper 3. Alkaline pH

3. Rich Sources

🍊 Citrus Fruits 🍓 Berries 🍈 Guava 🫑 Capsicum 🥬 Green Leafy Veggies
EXAM HOTSPOT

Vitamin C + Iron Synergy

Dietary Ascorbic Acid INCREASES absorption of Iron when ingested simultaneously!

Toxicity note: Large doses are considered non-toxic in lecture.

6. Vitamin C Deficiency — SCURVY

Result of prolonged deprivation of dietary Vitamin C.

1. Weakness
2. Pale Skin
3. Poor Appetite
4. Swollen Gums
5. Loosening Teeth
6. Bone/Joint Disease
