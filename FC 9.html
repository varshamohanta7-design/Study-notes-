1 REACTIONS OF FATS & FAT STABILITY

  • Fat Stability Significance: Maintaining fat stability is critical to retain fresh taste and odour during storage & handling.
  • Unsaturation Effect: Fats containing substantial unsaturation (double bonds) in their fatty acid chains are highly UNSTABLE.
⚡ Pro-oxidants:

Agents or factors that CAUSE or accelerate oxidation of fats (e.g. heat, UV light, transition metals like $Fe^{2+}$, $Cu^{2+}$).

🛡️ Antioxidants:

Agents that PREVENT or retard oxidation of fats (e.g. Tocopherol / Vit E, Ascorbic acid / Vit C).

2 WHAT IS RANCIDITY?

📌 Definition: Complete or incomplete oxidation OR hydrolysis of fats & oils when exposed to air, light, moisture, or by bacterial action, resulting in an unpleasant taste and bad odour.

Four Major Pathways of Rancidity:

Fresh Substrate
I. Hydrolytic Reaction with $H_2O$ + Enzymes/Heat
II. Oxidative Reaction with $O_2$ (Free Radicals)
III. Microbial Action of Microorganisms
IV. Ketonic Fungi on short-chain fatty acids

📌 EXAM TIP / MEMORY TRICK

Remember H-O-M-K for Rancidity Types:
• Hydrolytic ($H_2O$)
• Oxidative ($O_2$)
• Microbial (Microbes)
• Ketonic (Fungi/Ketones)

1. HYDROLYTIC RANCIDITY Pathway I

  • Core Mechanism: Triglycerides are hydrolysed when exposed to water, causing the release of Free Fatty Acids (FFA).
  • Catalysts Required: Reaction requires catalysts such as acids, bases, enzymes (lipase), or thermal effects (heat).
  • Off-Odour Source: Liberated short-chain free fatty acids are highly volatile and produce a strong, unpleasant smell.

A. Enzymatic Hydrolysis (Lipase Catalyzed):

B. Saponification (Base-Catalyzed Hydrolysis): IMP FOR EXAM

When a base (e.g., NaOH) acts as the hydrolysing agent, the liberated fatty acids are converted into their salts or SOAPS.

$$\text{Triglyceride} + 3\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{Glycerol} + 3\text{R-COO}^-\text{Na}^+ \text{ (SOAP 🧼)}$$
🧼 Note: Alkaline hydrolysis breaks ester bonds to yield soap salts directly.

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE TO AVOID

Don't confuse Hydrolytic Rancidity with Oxidative Rancidity!
• Hydrolytic requires WATER ($H_2O$) & cleaves ester bonds.
• Oxidative requires OXYGEN ($O_2$) & attacks double bonds!

2. OXIDATIVE RANCIDITY Pathway II

⚡ Associated with chemical spoilage by molecular oxygen ($O_2$) from air. The double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids are cleaved via a free radical chain reaction.

Chain Mechanism: 3 Stages

1. INITIATION STAGE Formation of Free Radical

The unsaturated hydrocarbon loses a hydrogen atom ($H^\bullet$) to form an alkyl free radical ($R^\bullet$).

$$RH \longrightarrow R^\bullet + H^\bullet$$
💡 Definition of Free Radical: An atom, molecule, or ion that possesses an unpaired electron (highly unstable & reactive!).
2. PROPAGATION STAGE Peroxide Chain Reaction

Atmospheric oxygen ($O_2$) reacts with free radical ($R^\bullet$) to form peroxide radicals ($ROO^\bullet$). These react further with more unsaturated fatty acids to produce new radicals, amplifying the chain reaction!

$$R^\bullet + O_2 \longrightarrow ROO^\bullet \text{ (Peroxide Radical)}$$
3. TERMINATION STAGE Non-Radical Products

Two radicals combine together to form a new single covalent bond, terminating the chain reaction.

$$ROO^\bullet + ROO^\bullet \longrightarrow ROR + O_2 \text{ (End Products)}$$

⚠️ Impact: The resulting end-products (aldehydes, ketones, polymers) are highly reactive and directly cause the unpleasant smell and bad taste of spoiled lipids.

3. MICROBIAL RANCIDITY Pathway III

  • Occurs when microorganisms such as molds or bacteria secrete enzymes like lipases to break down fat structures in oils.
  • Prevention Methods:
    • Inhibiting or destroying micro-organisms
    • Pasteurization
    • Addition of Antioxidants

🛡️ ANTIOXIDANTS

Substances added to lipid-containing foods to retard oxidative breakdown and prevent spoilage.

Mechanism: They donate an electron to combine with free radicals ($R^\bullet / ROO^\bullet$), thereby interrupting the free radical chain reaction!

Key Examples: 🍊 Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C) 💊 Tocopherol (Vitamin E)

4. KETONIC RANCIDITY Pathway IV

  • Encountered by the action of specific fungi: Aspergillus niger and blue-green mold Penicillium glaucum on oil seeds.
  • Fungal enzymes turn oils/fats into short-chain fatty acids, which are converted into a homologous series of aliphatic methyl ketones (e.g. pentan-2-one, heptan-2-one, nonan-2-one) causing a tallowy odor.

📌 4 Essential Prerequisites for Ketonic Rancidity:

(a) Presence of Molds
(b) Low Water Activity ($a_w$)
(c) Low partial pressure of $O_2$
(d) Presence of short-chain fatty acids

HYDROGENATION OF OILS

📌 Definition: Treatment of liquid unsaturated vegetable oil with Hydrogen gas ($H_2$) in the presence of a catalyst (Nickel/Palladium/Platinum) to decrease double bonds ($C=C$) and increase single bonds ($C-C$).

Converts liquid vegetable oils into solid fats (complete hydrogenation) or semi-solid fats (partial hydrogenation, e.g., Vegetable Ghee / Vanaspati).

Step-by-Step Mechanism of Hydrogenation:

Step 1
Adsorption of Hydrogen: Hydrogen gas ($H_2$) is adsorbed onto the surface of the metallic catalyst (e.g., Nickel, Ni).
Step 2
Activation of Hydrogen: $H_2$ molecules dissociate into individual reactive hydrogen atoms ($H^\bullet$) on the catalyst surface.
Step 3
Diffusion of Hydrogen Atoms: Hydrogen atoms diffuse into the unsaturated fatty acid molecule.
Step 4
Addition Across Double Bond: Hydrogen atoms add across the double bond ($C=C$), causing the double bond to break and form single bonds.
Step 5
Saturated Fatty Acid Formation: Result is a fully saturated fatty acid with no double bonds.
General Reaction Equation:
$$R-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-R + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Heat, Nickel Catalyst (Ni)}} R-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-R$$

TRANS FATS, MARGARINE & ADVANTAGES

💔 Production of Trans Fats (Side Reaction)

Partial chemical hydrogenation (which does not fully saturate all double bonds) causes geometric isomerization forming Trans Fatty Acids.

⚠️ Health Risk: Trans fats raise LDL (bad cholesterol), lower HDL, and significantly increase cardiovascular health risks!

🧈 Margarine Production in Food Industry

Hydrogenation is widely used to stabilize and solidify liquid vegetable oils when added to margarine to achieve specific textural, spreading, and melting properties desired by consumers.

Key Advantages of Hydrogenation:

🛡️ Higher Stability Hydrogenated oils are significantly more stable against oxidation than liquid unsaturated oils.
💰 Cheap Substitute Serves as an affordable alternative to expensive animal fats like lard or natural butter.
🌡️ Increased Melting Point Changes physical state from liquid oil to solid/semi-solid fat at room temperature (e.g., Vegetable Ghee).

🎯 QUICK REVISION CHEAT-SHEET FOR EXAM:

• Hydrolytic Rancidity: $H_2O$ + Lipase ⟶ Free Fatty Acids + Glycerol

• Saponification: Triglyceride + NaOH ⟶ Glycerol + Soap

• Oxidative Rancidity: Unsaturated Fat + $O_2$ ⟶ Free Radicals ($RH \rightarrow R^\bullet \rightarrow ROO^\bullet$)

• Microbial Rancidity: Bacteria/molds lipase activity; stopped by Antioxidants (Vit C & E)

• Ketonic Rancidity: Aspergillus niger ⟶ Methyl ketones (Pentan-2-one)

• Hydrogenation Catalyst: Nickel (Ni), Palladium (Pd), Platinum (Pt)
