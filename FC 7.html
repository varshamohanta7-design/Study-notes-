📌 CLASSIFICATION OF PROTEINS

1. Based on Chemical Composition
Protein Class Sub-type Characteristics Examples / Occurrence
Globular Proteins Albumins Soluble in water, dilute salt solution, dilute acid and base; coagulated by heat. Lactalbumin, egg albumin, serum albumin.
Globulins Soluble in salt solution, insoluble in H₂O. Serum globulin, arachin & conarchin of peanuts, myosin.
Histones Basic proteins, soluble in most common solvents. Nucleoproteins.
Fibrous / Scleroproteins Collagens Resistant to digestive enzymes, insoluble. Skin, tendons, bones.
Elastins Partially resistant to digestive enzymes. Arteries, tendons, elastic tissues.
Keratin Highly insoluble and resistant to digestive enzymes. Skin, hair, nails.
  • Globular Proteins: Compact, spherical structure. Highly responsive to changes in pH, ionic strength, and temperature.
  • Fibrous Proteins (Scleroproteins): Long polypeptide chains forming fibers. Play major structural roles in animal body tissue.

🎯 EXAM TIP (ICAR JRF)

• Casein is a Phosphoprotein in Milk.
• Mucin is a Glycoprotein in Saliva.
• Lipovitellin is a Lipoprotein in Egg Yolk.
• Ceruloplasmin contains Copper (Cu) as a metalloprotein!

2. Classification Based on Conformation
  • ✏️ Simple Proteins: Yield ONLY amino acids on complete hydrolysis.
    ➔ Examples: Albumin, Globulin, Prolamine etc.
  • ✏️ Conjugated Proteins: Made up of simple protein (apoprotein) + non-protein substance known as prosthetic group. On hydrolysis, yield amino acids PLUS other organic/inorganic compounds.
  • ✏️ Derived Proteins: Degradation products obtained by action of acids, alkalis, or enzymes on simple/conjugated proteins.
    ➔ Examples: Denatured proteins, Peptones, Polypeptides.

3. Classification Based on R-Group or Side Group
• Acidic Amino Acids: Contain more carboxyl (-COOH) groups than amino (-NH₂) groups.
➔ Eg: Aspartic acid, Glutamic acid.
• Basic Amino Acids: Contain more amino (-NH₂) groups than carboxyl (-COOH) groups.
➔ Eg: Lysine, Glutamine, Histidine.
• Neutral Amino Acids: Equal number of amino and carboxyl groups.
➔ Eg: Glycine, Alanine, Serine.
• Sulphur Containing: Contain sulphur (S) atoms.
➔ Eg: Methionine, Cysteine.

• Aromatic Amino Acids: Contain aromatic ring (ring form).
➔ Examples: Phenylalanine, Tyrosine, Tryptophan.

DENATURATION, COAGULATION & FOOD SOURCES

Class 07 • Protein Chemistry & Single Cell Proteins (SCP)
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE

Students think peptide bonds break during denaturation. NO!
• Breakage occurs in: Hydrogen bonds, Disulphide bonds & Hydrophobic interactions.
• PEPTIDE BONDS REMAIN INTACT!

🔥 DENATURATION OF PROTEINS

• Definition: Denaturation is a process in which a protein loses its native shape due to the disruption of weak chemical bonds and interactions, thereby becoming biologically inactive. It leads to loss of physical, chemical and structural properties.

⚙️ Mechanism & Agents:

- Unfolding of native protein occurs by physical and chemical agents.
- Higher Temperature: Heat denaturation / Thermal denaturation.
- Lower Temperature: Cold denaturation.

🚨 NO BREAKAGE OF PEPTIDE BONDS occurs during denaturation! Only H-bonds, disulfide bonds, and hydrophobic interactions break.

• Causes of Denaturation: Heat, violent shaking or agitation, hydrostatic pressure, UV radiation, acids & alkalis, organic solvents etc.

🧪 COAGULATION OF PROTEINS

• Coagulation: Denaturation can result in coagulation, which is the clotting or curdling of protein molecules into a relatively insoluble protein mass.

Flowchart: Sequence of Protein State Change

• Factors Affecting Coagulation:

1. Acid: Denaturation occurs more readily under acidic conditions.

2. Mechanical action (beating, whipping).

3. Sugar: Delays coagulation.

4. Salt: Promotes coagulation.

5. Rennin Enzyme: Promotes curdling/coagulation.

➔ Example: Egg white turns from transparent liquid to white solid coagulum upon cooking!

🥩 Nature of Food Proteins

Animal Proteins:

  • Meat: Edible muscle (cattle, sheep, swine) = 20-25% protein (wet basis).
  • Fish: Contains 10-21% protein.
  • Egg: 13-14% protein. White = Ovalbumin; Yolk = Lipoprotein & Phosphoprotein.
  • Milk: Cow milk = ~3.5% protein (Casein accounts for 80%, Whey protein 20%).

Plant Proteins:

• Vegetables: 1-6% | Cereals: 7-15% | Pulses: > 20%

🧫 Single Cell Protein (SCP)

Microbes used due to rapid growth, high yield & controlled conditions.

  • 🍄 Yeast Species:
    • Candida utilis (Torula yeast)
    • Saccharomyces carlsbergensis (Brewer's yeast)
    ➔ Contain ~50% protein (dry basis).
  • 🌿 Algae Genera:
    • Chlorella (Green algae) & Spirulina (Blue-green algae)
    ➔ Contain 50-60% protein (dry weight basis).
  • 🌱 Fungi & plant leaf proteins are also extracted nowadays.
